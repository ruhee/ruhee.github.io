If I've known you awhile you may remember my silly little baseball website from 2015, the [David Price Nickname Generator](https://nationalpost.com/sports/baseball/mlb/toronto-blue-jays-fan-created-a-david-price-nickname-generator-and-the-ace-thought-it-was-pretty-funny). It happened at a good time for Jays content, when they were in the division race and gunning for their first playoff appearance in an eternity, and it's had incredible staying power—I got a job partially off the back of that website, and many people I've met years later have been surprised to learn that that was me (including a new colleague I met only a few months ago!). 

This year I was inspired to make another dumb baseball thing, specifically after observing starting pitcher Dylan Cease switch away from the substantial beard he'd been sporting and back to the giant &rsquo;stache in time to have a [pretty bad outing](https://www.baseball-reference.com/boxes/ATH/ATH202609070.shtml) against the Athletics. Of course I thought: are those things related? And now we have a website for it, which is called, of course, [STACHECAST](https://stachecast.lol/).

It came out in mid-September and it's been incredibly fun—people think it's funny, which is all I wanted, and it's actually taught me a lot about a number of things, including modern frontend (I'm using [Parcel](https://parceljs.org/) and it's been cool, except for its wildly aggressive local cache), data visualization and when _not_ to use line charts, the rhythm of MLB strikeout video compilations (there's almost always a warmup shot, which is very helpful to me), and Cease's pitch arsenal, among others.

I am, however, creeping myself out while researching (do you know how many screenshots you need in order to accurately compare across years like this???). Cease is a handsome guy and all, but there's a limit to how much I can comfortably look at a stranger's face and I've blown _right_ past it. Data collection for prior seasons is on hold for a bit, lol.

I also went on tour this month! My band [Survival Club](https://survivalclubtheband.com/) opened six Ontario and Quebec dates in support of [The Honest Heart Collective](https://honestheart.co/), who are touring their new album _Sheer Will_. We played Sudbury, Ottawa, Kingston, Montréal, London, and Toronto in the span of about 9 days and I had a great time. You definitely get better _fast_ when you're playing every night, and I enjoyed being able to give myself little notes after the show that I tried to act on the next night.

(One night in Kingston I noticed myself resolving a vocal harmony to the V chord too early, and it's wild that I was singing, playing keyboards, _and_ thinking about this in such specific detail at the same time on stage. Offloading things to muscle memory can get weird!)
